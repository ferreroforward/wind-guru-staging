# Wind Guru knowledge (reviewed and approved by Guillermo)

This file is what Wind Guru AI knows beyond the live numbers. Guillermo edits it; nothing goes in without his approval. Last reviewed: approved by Guillermo on Oct 21 2026.

## The area and the spots

Wind in knots, distances in km, temperatures in Celsius, tides and waves in feet. Directions are where the wind blows from.

### Howe Sound

**Squamish (Spit and Nexen Beach).** Advanced. Wing foil, kite, windsurf. Rides on the summer and fall inflow thermal, a S to SW wind (about 150 to 260 degrees) that builds in the afternoon when the valley heats up. Never recommend Squamish on an outflow: any wind with north in it (about 271 to 89 degrees) is outflow, cold air draining down the Sound. Riders do not launch on an outflow; it is only worth it by boat. Say clearly that it is an outflow, not an inflow. The Spit wind meter is off from mid September to April, so there is no free live reading at the Spit in fall and winter; Until the Spit meter is back, the live wind at Squamish comes from the shipping terminal sensor at the head of the Sound (published by Kiteloop): it shows the outflow and the inflow, but it is not on the Spit, so say so. Squamish Airport (EC wsk) sits in a wind shadow and reads low, use it for temperature and pressure, not wind.

**Porteau Cove.** Intermediate. Windsurf and wing foil. Read it through Pam Rocks (EC was) at the Howe Sound entrance. It works on an inflow (Pam Rocks from about 130 to 230 degrees) of 14kt or more, or on an outflow (north) of 22kt or more at Pam Rocks. An established outflow reaches the cove a bit lighter: Pam Rocks 22 is about 12 at the cove, 26 about 22, 30 about 27. Pam Rocks 22 to 25 in outflow was a 4m day with great swell (Oct 11). A flooding tide can kill the swell even when the wind still works.

### English Bay and the North Shore

**Jericho and Spanish Banks.** Beginner friendly. Windsurf, wing foil, kite. Works from about SW to NW (230 to 335 degrees). Jericho Sailing Centre is here. Can foil at any tide, but below about 10ft it is a long walk to the water. For wind right now, use the Jericho Sailing Centre's own station on the beach (half hour average and gust). The English Bay buoy (EC 46304) is open water well off the beach and is often much stronger: on Oct 20 at 7am to 8am the buoy read W 12 to 14 while the beach was under 1kt. Earlier notes said the forecast runs about 4kt low here, but that was measured against the buoy, not the beach, so it is not settled.

**Dundarave Pier Beach.** Intermediate. Windsurf, wing foil, kite. Works SW to NW (230 to 320 degrees). Forecast has run low here (often 5kt or more under the English Bay buoy, partly because the buoy is open water).

**Ambleside Beach.** Intermediate. Windsurf and wing foil. Works SW to NW (230 to 320 degrees). Port rules: no sail, kite, wing or paddle craft in the First Narrows shipping zone, which starts just off Ambleside toward the Lions Gate Bridge. Stay west and close to shore. Forecast has run low here, like Dundarave.

**Erwin Park.** Intermediate. Windsurf, wing foil, kite. Works E to SE (90 to 140 degrees) and on a W (260 to 280). Scored against Point Atkinson (EC wsb), which reads stronger than the beach. Kites can only launch at low tide (very small beach); tides do not matter for wing, foil or windsurf.

**Cates Park Boat Launch (North Vancouver).** Intermediate. Wing foil and windsurf. Works E through W (90 to 280 degrees); anything with north in it is offshore. Hazards: rocks by the beach next to the ramp at low tide when coming in or out on the foil, and lots of boat traffic heading to Indian Arm and Deep Cove. Port rules: no sail, kite, wing or paddle craft in the Second Narrows shipping zone, about 2km west of the ramp (a line north from Berry Point), so do not ride west toward the bridges. 5 knot limit within 150m of shore around the park and launch: get out past 150m before getting up on the foil. No tide limit, deep water at the ramp. No live station.

### Fraser Delta and South Delta

**Steveston (Garry Point Park).** Never the pick. Wind Guru does not recommend it; it is shown only for experienced local riders. Port rules: wind powered craft are not allowed in the Fraser River shipping channel or within 61m of it, heavy commercial boat traffic, always give way. Expert only, know self rescue, never ride alone. If asked, say it is not recommended and point to another spot. Sand Heads (EC wvf) is its station.

**Tsawwassen Ferry Terminal Causeway.** Advanced only, high consequences if things go wrong, especially kiting. Sides: SE (120 to 150) works on the south side but gusty; S to SW (151 to 260) south side onshore; W (261 to 284) side shore on both sides; NW to N (285 to 30) south side offshore (advanced only) and north side onshore, but the north side needs 10ft or more of tide (it dries below that) and has little room to launch a kite. South side is not foilable below about 8ft of tide. Ferry Terminal station (EC vtf).

**Boundary Bay (Centennial Beach).** Beginner friendly. Kite, wing foil, windsurf. Works NE to S (45 to 180 degrees). Foil sports need a tide of about 10ft or more; kiting and windsurfing work at any tide. No live station.

**White Rock East Beach.** Beginner friendly. Kite, wing foil, windsurf. Works E through south to WNW (90 to about 305 degrees): a WNW wraps around the peninsula and is sometimes very good (Guillermo, Oct 22 2026); a strong NW can also wrap in, a local judgment call. Kiting needs a tide under about 12ft (above that it is nearly impossible to launch); winging, parawinging and windsurfing work at any tide. The city weather station has been offline.

**Crescent Beach.** Beginner friendly. Kite, wing foil, windsurf. Works SW to W (210 to 280) and on a N (345 to 10). Same kite tide limit as White Rock East (under about 12ft).

### General riding knowledge
When the tide runs against the wind, waves get steeper and choppier. Wind blowing all night builds bigger waves by morning. When salmon are running in the Fraser River, they can hit foils near the river mouth.

## Squamish go or no go (fall)

The Spit thermal needs the morning outflow to die and the valley to warm up. Check these in the morning:

1. Pam Rocks trend 7am to 11am. Outflow fading each hour, going calm, then SE by noon to 1pm is a GO sign. Outflow still building, or 20kt or more at noon, is NO GO.
2. Pressure: Pemberton (wgp) minus Vancouver (yvr). Level or falling about 1 hPa per hour is good. Pemberton still 3 hPa or more above Vancouver and not moving is NO GO.
3. Temperature: Squamish Airport (wsk) 2 to 3 degrees warmer than Pam Rocks by 1pm to 2pm is a GO sign. Sun alone is not enough (Oct 15: sunny, warm valley, but the outflow held and the thermal never came).
4. A light northerly model wind is not proof there is no outflow. Use the Pam Rocks trend and the pressure gap as the switch, not the forecast speed.

Late go: if by noon the outflow is clearly collapsing (Pam Rocks dropping each hour), the pressure lead is falling about 1 hPa per hour, and the valley has caught up with Pam Rocks, call it a late go with the thermal from about 4pm to 7pm, not a no go (Oct 16: no go calls at 10:30am, 11:48am and 12:30pm were wrong; it blew 15 to 20 from 4:30pm to 7pm).

October pattern: on sunny October days after a morning outflow, the thermal has come in stronger (about 2 to 7kt above the forecast) and later (1 to 3 hours) than the page says, and often lasts past 5pm when the page fades it. Tally so far: 6 days fit the pattern, 1 counter case (Oct 15). Say this as a tendency, not a promise.

Inflow day missed as outflow (Oct 10): on a sunny day after a front, a W to NW Strait wind (Point Atkinson W 10kt or more) can feed the Spit thermal even while the models all say north. Same day tell: Pam Rocks turns from N to SE by noon with Point Atkinson W 10kt plus sun.

## Where the forecast tends to be off

Strait spots with a learned blend (Steveston, Tsawwassen, Erwin, Porteau through Pam Rocks) are the most trustworthy; see the trust labels on the page. Jericho, Dundarave and Ambleside use a plain model average; against the English Bay buoy they ran low, but the buoy is stronger than the beaches, and Jericho is now being checked at the beach station. Squamish uses a learned thermal trained on summer data; in October it starts too early, peaks too low and fades too soon. Porteau in a morning outflow: the page number can run low, check Pam Rocks live. Environment Canada's marine forecast calling stronger than the page has been wrong most days for the Strait spots and closer for English Bay and Howe Sound outflow.

## How to read the page

Each hour shows the odds (percent) of getting the rider's chosen wind range, 12kt and up by default. Green is good odds, yellow maybe, red unlikely. "Learned" or MOS means a blend of models fitted to a year of Environment Canada station data; when it says 70%, it has happened about 70% of the time. The EC marine note is the official marine forecast for that zone. The swell index rates waves (flat, chop, waves, good swell) from wind, fetch and tide. Grey cells marked OUTFLOW at Squamish mean an outflow riders do not launch on. Guillermo's take is his own read on a day, not the model. The track record panel shows how each scored spot did over the last 30 days. Riders can tap an hour and use "Report actual conditions" to help tune it.
